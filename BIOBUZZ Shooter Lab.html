      Step 3 of 5 · team data

      Measure the real robot

      The simulator is only as good as its inputs. Measure the robot's fixed dimensions, then film shots to find the two numbers nobody can guess: transfer efficiency η and spin.

      Done when

        A · Robot setup

        Measure with a tape and protractor. Launch height is where the ball leaves the hood, measured from the tiles.

        B · Exit speed and spin from slow-motion video

        1. Tape two marks on a board behind the ball's path, 12 in apart, starting just after the hood.
        2. Put a stripe on a ball with a marker so you can count turns.
        3. Spin up the flywheel, read the RPM from telemetry, and film the shot in slow motion (240 fps if your phone has it).
        4. Step through the video and count the frames the ball takes between the marks, and how many turns the stripe makes in those frames.
        5. Repeat at 3 or more different RPMs. Five or more rows give a trustworthy η.

              Geometry and assumptions.
              • Blue HIVE from the BIOBUZZ Competition Manual (Fig. 9-10, section 9.6): pivot 43.95 in, CELLs 12.04 in deep with an 18.84 in gap, tipped 30°, opening 53.5–65.6 in above the tiles, A-frame base 38.95 in, HIVE at field center.
              • POLLEN ≈ 2.8 in, NECTAR ≈ 3.6 in (manual). Masses 24.9 g and 41.3 g come from a community model, so weigh your own.
              • 2D model. Drag Cd = 0.45, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³. Magnus CL ≈ S/(1+2S) with constant spin. Spin factor 1 means the ball rolls along the hood without slipping (ω = v/r). "Rising entry" means the ball's path is more than 60° off the CELL's axis as it crosses the opening.
